# Research reuse and scene review — November 16, 2026

This release started with 1,000 current games and a clean Git working tree at
`1ea3d2eb581cf2dfca5d91c063eafd8e0c9cc19a`. The user requested **200 additional
independent games**, rather than another 500-game expansion.

## Sources reused

No new search queries, keyword reports, or search screenshots were collected.
The earlier handover's record of 13 reports and twelve search samples is retained
in [sources.json](../expansion-1000/sources.json) and
[search-intent.md](../expansion-1000/search-intent.md). The original spreadsheet
and screenshot attachments are absent from this machine; this release does not
claim to have parsed those originals. Missing traffic metrics remain unknown.

The [audience standard](../../audience-content-standard.md),
[review ledger](../audience-review-2026-11-14.csv), and
[context decisions](../context-decisions-2026-11-14.csv) informed the new scenes.
Existing name/context prompts remain documented; preserving an earlier game
does not certify its difficulty.

## How the scenes were checked

The full 1,000-title inventory was read before the new plan was compiled.
Candidate word pools were compared with the actual selected answers in earlier
boards. Similarity led to a closer reading of the scenes, not an automatic claim
of originality or ease. The final report compares every new selected answer set
with all earlier old and new games; board geometry is checked separately.

Examples of changes during scene selection:

- Repeated tomato/chicken soup scenes gave way to white-bean soup, hummus with
  pita, and a filled corn tortilla, with the dish's own defining words retained.
- Another shoe-fitting scene became a watch-battery visit. Another glasses-frame
  scene became buying a roll of postage stamps.
- Another raking-leaves scene became a plant cutting rooting in water.
- A second community garden plot visit became borrowing a shared tool.
- Another card-counter lunch became a catalog order at a general store.
- Another checkers promotion became a Connect Four game.
- Another clothesline scene became a garden center's opening display.
- Previously covered Bible basket, Abraham-welcome, Samuel-call, and lost-coin
  scenes were replaced with distinct passages and objects. Hannah's coat visit
  differs from Samuel's nighttime call; the lake breakfast differs from feeding
  the crowd with loaves and fish.
- The already covered bicycle-for-two song became Row, Row, Row Your Boat.

Each scene has three explicitly required, explained answers. They must all occur
in its final board. Supporting vocabulary stays in the setting rather than
introducing specialist words just to obtain crossings. The step-cleaning scene
does not need the stair term RISER; the flashlight scene does not need the British
synonym TORCH. Neither is selected in the final release. Garden flower names,
named games, foods, and optional Bible names have direct descriptions and, where
useful, first-letter hints. The title itself identifies the chosen interest.

The source plan, context-specific clues and 600 answer notes are separately
reviewable in `scripts/expansion-1200-*.txt`. The compiler resolves the ordinary
supporting definitions into the 40 source JSON banks. Reused words such as bank,
bed, coat, roll, bill, needle, and light were checked in their scene's meaning.
Singular/plural and action/object senses remain distinct. Exact-clue uniqueness
within a series supplements this review; paraphrasing alone is not a new scene.

This was model-assisted writing and review, not professional crossword editing,
a study with older US readers, a medical intervention, or a ranking prediction.
Reader difficulty can still vary with interests and vocabulary.
