# Audience and content standard

Updated November 14, 2026 from the user's explicit direction: prioritize easy, older-reader-friendly, large-print, printable, themed puzzles, rather than catalog size. The current 500 is a published inventory, not evidence that 500 puzzles meet this standard.

## What a useful puzzle offers

1. A recognizable scene: a garden, kitchen, small-town shop, familiar old song, family custom, daily routine, or optional basic Bible story.
2. Mostly familiar answers that can be found from the clue without outside research. Long everyday words can be easier than short specialist words; familiarity depends on context.
3. Short, direct clues in ordinary American English. No new internet slang, memes, celebrity-news references, elaborate exclusions, or technical definitions used to disguise obscure answers.
4. Correct meanings, parts of speech and number. Ambiguity is considered alongside crossing letters. Simplification must preserve accuracy.
5. A coherent grid and answer selection, useful solving guidance, working hints/save behavior, and readable puzzle and answer print sheets.

Detailed introductions, unique clue wording, exact-pair uniqueness and valid grids do not by themselves establish reader value. The site must not describe model-generated content as hand-authored or claim professional or target-reader validation that did not occur.

## Theme priorities

| Theme | Favor | Avoid |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Gardening | Familiar flowers, tools, watering, seasonal chores | Anatomy terms such as CYATHIUM or CUPULE used as easy answers |
| Nostalgia and small towns | Familiar shops, household objects, mail, local gatherings | Obscure machine parts or museum terminology |
| Old songs | Recognizable tunes, ordinary title words, common instruments | Deep artist biographies, discography dates, difficult music theory |
| Everyday wellbeing | Rest, walking, daily routines and ordinary food vocabulary | Treatment instructions, diagnostic puzzles, memory-benefit claims |
| Bible | Optional, clearly identified basic stories and familiar names | Denominational tests, obscure chapter/verse recall, assumed beliefs |

The existing Christmas Nativity scene is not a full Bible collection. There is no dedicated everyday-wellbeing collection yet. Do not create empty landing pages or rename unrelated games to imply coverage. Older adults are a varied audience; interest in one period, religion or activity is not universal.

## Match search intent with the real experience

`Easy`, `printable`, `large print` and `themed` describe specific needs. Satisfy those needs with the actual vocabulary, clue design, print size and themed collection. Their presence in a title is not a ranking weight or an indexing guarantee. One useful page can satisfy closely related free/printable/with-answers searches without duplicate keyword pages.

Use supplied research and actual US Search Console query/page data to prioritize topics. Do not assume that 1,000 pages will outperform a smaller collection, or that a root query proves demand for every subtheme. Reader completion, requests for help, ambiguous-clue reports and printing feedback should inform editorial work when available. No new analytics collection is authorized or implemented by this document.

## Existing-catalog review

The [500-row ledger](research/audience-review-2026-11-14.csv) separates:

- `replace-vocabulary`: a selected answer matches a curated list of especially specialist vocabulary; review or replace it for the general easy collection.
- `review-context`: topic-dependent vocabulary or substantial name/title recall needs a closer reading and possible simplification.
- `candidate-retain`: no listed risk was detected; this does not mean a reader has validated the puzzle as easy.

This is model-assisted triage, not independent professional editing, a complete factual audit of every clue, a word-frequency classification, or testing with older US readers. The listed vocabulary is an editorial prompt, not an exhaustive dictionary. The ledger records actual selected answers and clues rather than unused word-bank candidates. Long words alone do not trigger a flag.

Twenty-two wording corrections in thirteen games are recorded in the [clue change ledger](research/audience-clue-changes-2026-11-14.json). They remove a circular old-song clue and simplify science, gardening and sports language while preserving every answer, layout, ID and progress key. They do not resolve the specialist-answer backlog. Source word banks are updated along with the displayed clues.

The next content work should address the flagged answers and name-heavy clues before further numeric expansion. When replacing a board, preserve its earlier edition and progress association. Do not erase saved progress or quietly put a different grid under the same edition key.
